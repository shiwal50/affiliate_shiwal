If you are running a white-label agency on HighLevel's SaaS mode, you have likely run into this scenario: a local business client goes wild with automated features, runs up a massive bill on AI tools, and then treats the resulting invoice like a surprise tax. Until now, managing heavy usage meant either absorbing the sudden platform costs yourself or getting into awkward arguments after a feature stops working entirely.

HighLevel has just rolled out SaaS usage limits via Labs, and it completely changes how agencies handle client consumption. You can now set hard or soft caps on features like Voice AI and Conversation AI for every individual sub-account on your roster.

### How Client SaaS Limits Work in Practice

When onboarding a new client or managing an existing one, you can configure consumption caps directly in the SaaS configurator. If a client exhausts their monthly allocation, you have the flexibility to either bill them for the overage or halt the specific action until their billing cycle resets.

Transparency is built right into the client dashboard. Each sub-account billing page now features a clear consumption meter with color-coded warnings as they approach their limit. They can see exactly what they have used and when their counter refreshes. 

Even better, when a client hits a ceiling and cannot upgrade their plan tier, they can request a higher limit with a single click from their dashboard. You receive both an in-app and email notification, allowing you to approve the bump and automatically bill them for the extra volume, or reject it with a customized note—all without leaving your agency view.

### Why This Matters for Agency Margins

For agencies selling software-as-a-service, profitability lives and dies by predictable margins. Uncapped AI and voice features are powerful selling points, but a single rogue client can quickly erode your monthly recurring revenue if their usage spikes out of control.

By establishing clear boundaries, you turn potential billing disputes into structured upsell conversations. When a client outgrows their baseline limit, it is no longer a crisis; it is a clear signal that they are driving value from the tool and are ready to pay for more.
