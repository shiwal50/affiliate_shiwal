For marketing agencies running multi-channel campaigns, attribution has always been the thorniest part of call tracking. You set up a number pool, calls start rolling in for your local service or e-commerce clients, and half of them show up in the CRM labeled vaguely as 'Direct' or just a generic 'Facebook.' Proving exact ROI to clients becomes an uphill battle when your phone leads lose their digital footprint the moment someone taps a dial button.

HighLevel just rolled out a quiet update to number pool call tracking that solves this headache permanently. Call tracking now correctly labels Facebook ad calls as 'Facebook Paid,' eliminating the ambiguity that used to plague client reporting. But it doesn't stop there. The update brings native platform-level tagging across the board, covering TikTok, LinkedIn, Microsoft (Bing) ads, and even Google ads clicked directly inside mobile apps.

### Why This Matters for Client Retention

When you manage ad spend across Meta, Google, and LinkedIn simultaneously, clients want to know which platform is actually generating phone calls—not just form fills. Previously, if a prospect clicked a Facebook ad, browsed the site, and called ten minutes later, the attribution often broke or defaulted to direct traffic. You were left defending your agency's value with incomplete data.

Now, tagged links using standard UTM parameters (like `utm_medium=cpc`) automatically map to the correct source, appending 'Paid' to the platform name—such as 'Bing Paid.' Even emerging traffic sources like ChatGPT and other AI referral tools get their own dedicated source tags. 

There is zero configuration required for this update. The number pool tracking scripts update automatically on their own across your sub-accounts. However, it is worth noting that these rules apply strictly to new calls moving forward, and if a visitor lands with multiple conflicting tracking parameters, HighLevel applies a strict priority waterfall: Google takes precedence, followed by Facebook, TikTok, and LinkedIn.

### Tying Attribution to Automation

Better attribution isn't just about pretty weekly reports; it changes how you trigger automations. When phone calls are accurately tagged by paid platform, you can segment your workflows instantly. A caller originating from a LinkedIn ad campaign can trigger a different CRM pipeline stage and follow-up sequence than someone who found the business via organic search.

If you have been piecing together third-party call attribution tools just to give your clients clear reporting on their ad spend, you can finally ditch the extra subscription.
