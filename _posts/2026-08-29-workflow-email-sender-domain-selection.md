For years, managing email deliverability inside a HighLevel sub-account felt like riding in a vehicle with a single set of brakes for all four wheels. If you ran a high-volume promotional blast on a sub-account, every single transactional email—from password resets and appointment reminders to payment receipts—rode on that exact same sender domain. When a cold outreach sequence or a poorly targeted promo campaign triggered spam complaints, it dragged down your entire sending reputation with it. Vital client notifications suddenly started hitting the spam folder, creating unnecessary fires for your agency support team.

HighLevel has finally solved this structural limitation by introducing workflow-level sender domain selection. You can now assign a specific verified dedicated domain directly inside individual workflow settings, completely decoupling your transactional flows from your marketing campaigns.

### How Workflow-Level Domains Change Your Setup

Previously, changing the sender address on a workflow only altered the display name people saw in their inbox. The underlying technical domain remained tied to whatever the sub-account used globally. Now, the sender domain dropdown inside Workflow Settings lets you explicitly isolate different types of communication.

Imagine you run a local medical clinic sub-account. You can easily configure appointment reminders, booking confirmations, and billing receipts to dispatch exclusively from a secure transactional subdomain like `mail.acmeclinic.com`. At the same time, your monthly promotional nurtures, cold outreach sequences, and event invitations can live entirely on a separate sending domain like `news.acmeclinic.com`.

If a promotional sequence suffers from low engagement or high bounce rates, the damage is strictly quarantined to the promotional domain. Your appointment reminders keep humming along undisturbed on their own isolated infrastructure, protecting your client's core operational communications from a single bad campaign.

### Practical Implementation for Marketing Agencies

This update also opens up a safer way to test new domains. Instead of cutting over an entire client account to a brand-new warmup domain all at once, you can spin up a single workflow using the new address, monitor its inbox placement for a couple of weeks, and scale up gradually.

To put this into practice today, make sure your dedicated domains are properly configured under your Email Services settings. Open up any workflow, navigate to the settings panel, select your preferred sender domain from the dropdown, and define your matching sender details. Remember that individual email actions will still override workflow-level settings if configured manually, so keep your configurations clean and consistent.
