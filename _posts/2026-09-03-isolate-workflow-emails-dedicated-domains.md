If you run an agency with multiple automated sequences firing from a single HighLevel sub-account, you have likely run into a frustrating bottleneck. Until recently, every single workflow in a given sub-account defaulted to sending out from the same global domain. 

That meant your transactional appointment reminders, billing receipts, cold outreach campaigns, and monthly promotional blasts all shared the exact same sender domain reputation. 

### The Risk of a Shared Domain Setup

When transactional messages and cold marketing emails live on the same domain, a single poorly performing campaign can jeopardize your entire operation. If a batch of promotional emails triggers spam complaints or hits low engagement thresholds, your domain's sender reputation takes a hit. Suddenly, critical appointment confirmations and password reset emails stop reaching your clients' inboxes.

For local businesses and service providers, missing a booking notification because a marketing blast went sideways is a massive headache. It introduces friction where there should be seamless automation.

### Granular Control in Workflow Settings

HighLevel has addressed this structural limitation by adding a dedicated sender domain dropdown directly into the Workflow Builder settings. Now, rather than relying solely on global email settings, you can assign a specific verified domain to individual workflows.

If you have multiple dedicated domains configured under your Email Services, you can segment your outbound traffic intentionally. For example, you can route high-value operational messages through a secure subdomain like `mail.yourclientdomain.com`, while keeping your aggressive marketing and nurture sequences on a completely separate domain.

If a marketing campaign underperforms, the fallout is entirely isolated to that specific domain. Your core operational workflows continue to land safely in the primary inbox without collateral damage.

### Practical Implementation for Agencies

Setting this up requires just a few basic steps:

1. Head over to **Email Services** and ensure your dedicated domains are fully authenticated and verified.
2. Open the specific workflow you want to isolate, navigate to **Settings**, and locate the new **Sender domain** dropdown.
3. Choose your desired domain, enter your preferred From name and email prefix, and save your changes.

Keep in mind that individual email action settings will still override these workflow-level rules, giving you a fallback mechanism if you need granular exceptions within a sequence.

This update is a subtle quality-of-life improvement, but it solves a very real risk for agencies managing high-volume client accounts.
