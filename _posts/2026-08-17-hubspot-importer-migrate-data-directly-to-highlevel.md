If you have ever tried migrating an agency client off HubSpot and into HighLevel via CSV exports, you know how painful it can be. Mapping fields, formatting dates, restructuring deals into opportunities, and praying that associations do not break usually takes hours of tedious manual cleanup. 

HighLevel has finally rolled out a native HubSpot Importer that handles this heavy lifting directly through a secure account connection. Instead of exporting flat files and guessing at data relationships, you can now link your accounts and move core CRM data across without leaving the platform.

### What Actually Moves Over

The integration goes beyond basic contact details. When you run an import, the tool supports:

*   **Contacts, Companies, and Deals:** Deals map neatly over as opportunities inside your specified pipelines and stages.
*   **Custom Fields:** Contact, company, and deal properties automatically migrate as custom fields.
*   **Activity Data:** Notes, emails, tasks, and supported record associations travel along with the core profiles.

Running the import is straightforward. Head over to your Contacts, Companies, or Opportunities view, click the import option, and choose the HubSpot connection prompt. Once you sign in and select the objects you want to bring across, the system runs the migration in the background. You can check the Import History table to track progress, review error warnings, and even download results as a CSV if you need to audit specific records.

### Practical Implications for Agency Migrations

For agencies that frequently onboard clients moving away from legacy tools, this feature drastically shortens deployment times. Client onboarding bottlenecks are often tied to data friction. When a business owner worries about losing historical notes, past deal stages, or custom data properties, they drag their feet on switching platforms.

By automating the pipeline and property mapping, your team can spin up a new sub-account in a fraction of the usual time. Of course, best practices still apply. You should always test your import in a non-production sub-account first to ensure custom property mappings look clean before running the live migration for a paying client.
