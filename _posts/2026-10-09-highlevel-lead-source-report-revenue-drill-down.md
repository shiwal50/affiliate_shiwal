If you manage client attribution inside HighLevel, you know the frustration of staring at a lead count total and having zero visibility into the actual pipeline value behind it. Until now, tracking ROI by channel meant running separate opportunity filters, exporting CSVs, or guessing which traffic sources were actually driving revenue versus just generating empty form fills.

HighLevel has rolled out a major update to the Lead Source Report widget, and it completely changes how agencies audit attribution data. You can now click directly on any count under Total Leads, Open, Won, Lost, or Abandoned to pull up the exact list of opportunities instantly. No more manual searching or navigating away from your dashboard just to figure out which client contact belongs to which conversion metric.

### Revenue Attribution by Status

Previously, your source breakdown gave you volume, but left you in the dark on monetary value until you dug deeper. The updated report now displays the exact dollar amount attached to Open, Won, Lost, and Abandoned statuses right inside the source table. For agency owners reporting back to local business clients, this is a massive win. You aren't just showing them that Facebook Ads brought in 40 leads; you can show them that those 40 leads represent $14,000 in open pipeline value and $4,500 in closed-won revenue.

### Cleaning Up Dirty Data

Every marketer knows that dirty data kills attribution models. Leads slip through without a source parameter due to tracking breaks, direct traffic quirks, or manual entry shortcuts. 

This update introduces two crucial data-hygiene features:

1. **The 'N/A' Row:** Opportunities that lack an assigned source are no longer silently omitted from your reporting table. They show up explicitly as "N/A," letting you spot data gaps immediately.
2. **Empty Source Filtering:** The Source filter now natively supports 'Is empty' and 'Is not empty' parameters. 

This means you can drop a segment onto your dashboard specifically designed to catch unassigned opportunities. Your team can review them, patch the missing UTM tags or form settings, and push them back into the correct attribution bucket before your monthly client review meeting.

### Practical Agency Implications

Most agencies struggle to prove ROI because channel reporting disconnects volume from closed revenue. When a client asks if their Google Ads spend is actually paying off, you need to show movement from initial click to closed invoice without spending hours cross-referencing contact records.

By leveraging these granular source reports, you can quickly identify wasted ad spend on channels that generate volume without pipeline value.
