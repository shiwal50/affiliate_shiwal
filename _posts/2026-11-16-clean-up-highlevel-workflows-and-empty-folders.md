If your agency has been using HighLevel for more than a few months, your automation architecture probably looks a bit like a junk drawer. We have all built quick test workflows, spun up experimental sequences for clients, and left half-finished folders sitting around in the backend. Over time, this digital clutter makes it genuinely difficult to audit your systems, onboard new team members, or prep accounts for client handovers.

HighLevel just rolled out a practical update inside Workflow Maps that tackles this exact problem. The new Cleanup feature gives agency operators a centralized way to identify dead weight, review inactive paths, and clear out empty folders without having to click through every single sub-folder manually.

### What the Cleanup Tool Actually Scans For

Instead of forcing you to hunt down forgotten sequences, the cleanup utility in Workflow Maps surfaces three specific categories of clutter:

1. **Default Workflow Names:** It highlights any automation that still carries a generic or default title. This is a lifesaver for identifying forgotten test sequences that were never properly labeled or documented.
2. **Inactive Workflows:** It flags any automation that has recorded zero enrollments over the past 60 days. While some of these might be seasonal campaigns you only run once a year, most of them are abandoned experiments that are just taking up visual space.
3. **Empty Folders:** It pinpoints directory folders that contain zero active workflows, allowing you to flatten your nested directory structure.

For growing agencies managing dozens of client sub-accounts, running routine maintenance like this is essential. A clean CRM backend means faster troubleshooting, fewer misfires when updating triggers, and a much smoother experience when handing an account over to a client or a new media buyer.

### Practical Implications for Agency Operations

Most agencies don't have a dedicated system administrator whose sole job is to audit automation setups. That means cleanup usually happens reactively—usually right when someone accidentally triggers an old test workflow on a live contact. 

By building a habit of checking the Cleanup tool during your monthly or quarterly account reviews, you can keep your automation maps lean. Just make sure your team double-checks the purpose of an inactive workflow before deleting it. A sequence might be quiet for two months simply because it serves a specific quarterly promotion or a niche client onboarding flow.

### Getting Started with Your First Cleanup

Running your first audit takes less than a minute. Head over to **Automation**, click into **Workflows**, and open up **Workflow Maps**. From there, look for the Cleanup option in the left rail. Inspect the candidates, verify that you aren't deleting anything tied to an active seasonal campaign, and confirm the removals to leave your account looking crisp, organized, and professional.
