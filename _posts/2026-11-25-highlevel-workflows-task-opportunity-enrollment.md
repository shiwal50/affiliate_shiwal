For years, building automated sequences in HighLevel meant everything had to revolve around a contact record. If an opportunity moved to a new pipeline stage, or a task was completed, you still needed a contact tied to that event to trigger most deep workflow actions. For agencies managing complex pipelines where deals or internal tasks live independently of a primary contact record, this created unnecessary workarounds.

HighLevel has rolled out a major update that changes how automation tracking works. Tasks and opportunities can now enroll in workflows entirely on their own, without requiring an associated contact. Alongside this capability, the platform has updated enrollment history and execution logs to support multi-object tracking.

### What This Means for Agency Operations

When deals move through your sales pipeline, they often require internal actions that do not map neatly onto a single lead. For instance, if an enterprise contract sits in review, you might want an automated sequence to track internal milestones, assign follow-up tasks, or notify account executives based strictly on the opportunity's properties.

Previously, troubleshooting these automations meant digging through contact timelines to figure out why a deal stalled. Now, you can inspect workflow activity directly at the object level. If a specific deal fails to trigger an action, you can search for that opportunity by name, review its exact execution log, and see which steps ran successfully.

The same applies to tasks. If your team relies on internal tasks for client onboarding or fulfillment, those tasks can now drive automation loops and maintain their own audit trails. This level of visibility makes it much easier to keep your internal processes tight without cluttering your contact databases with dummy records just to satisfy workflow triggers.

### How to Use Multi-Object Logs

Navigating these new logs requires a quick shift in how you filter your data inside the automation builder. When you open any workflow and jump into its enrollment history or execution logs, you will see a new object selector at the top.

Instead of a flat list, you can filter by:
- **All objects:** A combined view of every record currently moving through the automation.
- **Contacts:** Focusing strictly on traditional lead-based triggers.
- **Opportunities:** Isolating pipeline deals to check stage progressions and associated automation results.
- **Tasks:** Reviewing internal to-dos and their execution outcomes.

The search bar adapts dynamically depending on which object type you select. If you are investigating a stalled pipeline deal, switch to the opportunity filter, type the name of the deal, and immediately view its complete history.

### Practical Implementation for Growing Agencies

As your agency scales, automation complexity grows exponentially. Relying solely on contact-centric tracking can leave blind spots in your CRM data, especially when deals involve multiple stakeholders or heavy internal collaboration. By leveraging direct task and opportunity tracking, you can build cleaner, more resilient systems that reflect how your team actually works.
